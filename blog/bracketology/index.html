Weekend one is done. Time for Bracketology.

League play has started, so the tournament picture starts to mean something. Our first projected 64-team field, how it's built, and where the bubble sits today.

The first weekend of conference play is in the books (here’s how it went), and from here on every match counts twice: once in the standings and once toward the NCAA tournament. So today we’re opening a new tab: Bracketology.

How it works

We don’t pick the field by feel. We play out the rest of the season, over and over.

For every one of the 2,539 Division I matches still on the schedule, our power ratings give each team a chance to win. We simulate the whole regular season 1,000 times. At the end of each simulated season we calculate the RPI with the NCAA’s bonuses and penalties, then pick the field the way the committee’s manual describes:

  • 31 automatic bids, one per conference. Our stand-in for each league’s AQ is its simulated regular-season champion; we don’t simulate conference tournaments.
  • 33 at-large bids, taken in RPI order from teams at .500 or better. Where two teams are close, we compare them on the manual’s criteria: head-to-head, common opponents, and significant wins and losses.
  • The top 32 are seeded, and the top 16 host the first two rounds.

The bracket on the page is the most likely field from those simulations. Seeds 1–16 are placed on an S-curve, and seeds 17–32 and unseeded teams are placed as close to a host as the bracket rules allow. We then play that bracket out 10,000 times to get each team’s chance of reaching each round.

The four No. 1 seeds

Today’s projected top line is Nebraska, Pittsburgh, Louisville and Kentucky, and all four host a regional. Nebraska and Pittsburgh stand apart: each wins the title in about 37% of our runs, and each reaches the Final Four 84% of the time. Louisville (13%) and Kentucky (9%) are the only other teams above 1%.

The rest of the projected top 16, in order: SMU, Wisconsin, TCU, Arizona State, Indiana, Florida, Texas, Kansas, Washington, BYU, Tennessee and Purdue. Of those, only SMU hosts in every simulation. For everyone from Wisconsin (96%) down to Washington, Tennessee and Purdue (61% each), hosting is still up for grabs.

What it doesn’t know

The committee’s judgment, injuries, and conference tournament upsets aren’t modeled. It’s also September, and two months of results will move almost everything above. Treat it as where each team stands today and what the rest of its schedule looks like, not as a prediction of Selection Sunday.
