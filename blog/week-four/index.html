Week four: Florida wins twice in Texas, and the host line shuffles

The fourth weekend of league play in the seven conferences we track, and what it did to our projected NCAA tournament field: two new hosts, a tighter bubble, and an even bigger favorite.

Four weekends of conference play are done. Here’s what happened in the seven leagues we track, and what it did to the Bracketology projection.

The weekend in numbers

From Oct. 13 to Oct. 18 there were 95 conference matches in the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC, Big East, WCC and Mountain West. Forty-one were sweeps and 22 went five. Home teams won 50 of 95, after going 54-40 the week before. The lower-rated team in our power ratings won 20 times, and every league had at least one.

It’s the same story for a fourth straight week: the attack and the sideout decide matches, and serving and passing come a clear step behind. Five teams won despite losing the sideout battle, and four of them needed five sets. Georgetown’s win over Creighton is the best example. It sided out 60.7% to Creighton’s 64.0% and still won the fifth set 19-17.

Bracketology, week four

The top line held, and Nebraska pulled further ahead

The four projected No. 1 seeds are still Nebraska, Pittsburgh, Louisville and Kentucky. Nebraska now wins the championship in 59% of our simulations, up from 48% two weeks ago. Pittsburgh is at 20%, Louisville 12% and Kentucky 5%. Florida (2%) is the only other team above 1%.

Nebraska won at Northwestern and beat Purdue at home. Purdue took the second set, the first set Nebraska has lost in Big Ten play (24-1). Across the two matches the Huskers held opponents to a 26.5% kill rate and a .073 hitting percentage.

Florida made the biggest move among the seeded teams. It won at Texas A&M in four and at Texas in five, and it now hosts in every one of our simulations. Its average selection rank improved from about eighth to sixth.

Two new hosts

The top 16 host the first two rounds. This week two teams moved in and two moved out:

Baylor swept at TCU, 25-22, 25-22, 27-25, and now hosts in 66% of our runs, up from 46%. Minnesota beat Michigan in four and swept Maryland while holding the two to a .067 hitting percentage; it hosts in 63% of runs, up from 46%. Washington beat Rutgers but was swept at home by Indiana, a team ranked 17 spots lower in our power ratings, and its host chances fell from 51% to 30%. Texas beat Auburn and lost to Florida in five. Its host chances barely moved (53%), but Minnesota and Baylor passed it.

The host line is as crowded as it has been. Vanderbilt (71%), Minnesota (63%), Tennessee (62%), TCU (60%), Penn State (53%) and Texas (53%) are all close to even, and UCLA has jumped into the mix at 44%, up from 14%, after beating Penn State and Ohio State in five sets. Above them, the top 10 host in at least 98% of runs.

Georgia moved into the last four in, from 48% to 60%, after sweeping at Ole Miss and winning in five at LSU. Missouri is knocking: it swept Mississippi State and Alabama and rose from 31% to 42%. DePaul rose from 38% to 48% with two road wins. Going the other way, Oklahoma lost at home to Kentucky and Tennessee and dropped from 47% to 35%. The last at-large team now sits around No. 44 in the RPI, and in 80% of runs the cutoff falls between No. 40 and No. 47. Twenty-three teams make the field in at least 99% of runs, up from 20 a week ago. There are 1,506 D1 matches left to play.

Around the leagues

SEC

Kentucky (7-1) leads after winning at Oklahoma in four and sweeping at Arkansas. It sided out 68.6% with a 54.1% serve KO rate. Florida (6-1) won at Texas A&M and at Texas, 15-11 in the fifth, and passed 61.2% good across the two. Georgia (6-1) swept at Ole Miss with a 64.9% serve KO rate, then won in five at LSU. Vanderbilt beat Alabama in five and Mississippi State in four with a 46.0% kill rate, and Tennessee won at Arkansas and swept at Oklahoma.

Missouri swept Mississippi State and Alabama, holding them to a .143 hitting percentage. The win over Mississippi State, ranked eight spots higher, was the league’s only upset.

Fun fact: Both outsides had 30 kills when Georgia won in five at LSU: Bianna Muoneke for Georgia and Jurnee Robinson for LSU. Muoneke had 11 of hers in the third set, which LSU won 32-30.

Big Ten

Nebraska (8-0) leads Wisconsin and Oregon (both 7-1). Wisconsin swept Maryland and won at Iowa, siding out 73.4% with a 47.9% kill rate, the best offense in the league this week. Oregon beat Indiana in five and Rutgers in four. UCLA beat Penn State and Ohio State, both in five, with a 56.1% serve KO rate.

The Big Ten had five upsets, the most of any league: Indiana swept at Washington, Illinois beat Michigan State in four, UCLA beat Penn State, and Ohio State won at USC and Michigan won at Illinois, both in five. Northwestern (0-8, 4-24 in sets) is still winless.

Fun fact: UCLA libero Kat Lutz had 31 digs and four aces in the five-set win over Penn State. On the other side of the net, Kennedy Martin took 69 swings for 25 kills.

Big 12

BYU (7-0, 21-3 in sets) is the league’s only unbeaten team after sweeping at Utah and at Arizona with a 72.7% sideout rate. Arizona State (6-1) beat Arizona and then won in five at Kansas (5-2), which had swept at Kansas State earlier in the week. Colorado swept at Iowa State and passed 72.4% good.

Iowa State bounced back two days later to beat Utah 15-11 in the fifth. Utah went 0-2 for the week. That was one of three Big 12 upsets, along with UCF’s five-set win at Houston and West Virginia’s win over Cincinnati. Texas Tech (0-7) is still looking for its first league win.

Fun fact: Houston’s two outsides, Sydney Jones and Noemi Despaigne, combined for 48 kills against UCF (27 and 21) on 109 swings, and Houston still lost the fifth set 16-14.

ACC

Louisville (8-0, 24-1 in sets) swept Duke and North Carolina with a 59.9% serve KO rate and 68.8% good passing. Pittsburgh (7-1) swept Florida State and Miami and sided out 77.1%, the best of any team in these leagues with two matches this week. Georgia Tech swept Syracuse and Boston College with a 46.5% kill rate and a 9.5% ace rate.

The upset with the biggest bracket impact was Miami at SMU. The Hurricanes, No. 62 in our power ratings, won the fifth set 15-9 against the No. 9 Mustangs. SMU beat Florida State two days later and is still a projected host, but its average selection rank slipped from about eighth to ninth. Clemson over Boston College and Virginia Tech’s sweep of Virginia were the other ACC upsets. Syracuse is 0-8 with one set won.

Fun fact: Miami came back from two sets down at SMU and won the fourth set 33-31 to force a fifth. Ruth Martinez had 29 kills on 72 swings, and libero Gaby Arroyo had 30 digs.

Big East

Marquette (7-1) is alone at the top after sweeping at St. John’s and at Seton Hall. It held them to a .060 hitting percentage and passed 66.7% good. Creighton lost 19-17 in the fifth at Georgetown. DePaul (6-2) won at Seton Hall and in five at St. John’s, holding opponents to a 26.9% kill rate.

Providence was the only team in these leagues with two upsets this week: a sweep at Xavier and a five-set win at Butler. Seton Hall (0-8) is the league’s only winless team.

Fun fact: Georgetown libero Bella McGirr had 30 digs in the win over Creighton, which was decided 19-17 in the fifth.

WCC

Saint Mary’s (7-1) is alone at the top after winning two road matches in five sets, at San Francisco and at Santa Clara, where it was the lower-rated team. Pepperdine won at Loyola Marymount in four, and the two are a game back at 6-1. San Diego swept at Seattle and at Portland, siding out 69.6% and holding them to a .060 hitting percentage. Denver won in five at Portland, the league’s other upset.

Fun fact: Santa Clara’s Cate Shanahan had 26 kills in both of her matches this week, 52 in all on 127 swings.

Mountain West

Hawaii (8-0) won twice at Wyoming, in five and then in four, and held the Cowgirls to a 26.2% kill rate. UC Davis (5-3) split at San Jose State despite a 61.9% serve KO rate and an 11.4% ace rate for the weekend. UNLV swept Nevada and beat it again in four with a 61.7% serve KO rate.

Three of the 10 matches were upsets. San Jose State beat UC Davis in five, Air Force beat Grand Canyon in five a day after losing to them, and UTEP swept New Mexico before losing the rematch.

Fun fact: Air Force’s Alysa Walker had a double-double in both matches against Grand Canyon: 18 kills and 20 digs in the loss, then 23 kills and 23 digs in the five-set win. She made only one attack error in the second match.
