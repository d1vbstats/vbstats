Conference openers: who's 2-0 and why

The first weekend of league play in the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC, Big East, WCC and Mountain West, and the one number that decided almost every match.

League play opened across the seven conferences we track between Sept. 23 and 27: 91 conference matches, 42 of them sweeps. Road teams went 47-44, and there were upsets in every league except the Big East, which went exactly to form.

What decided the weekend

We have both teams’ charted numbers for all 91 matches. For each stat below, this is how often the team that won that stat also won the match:

Winning the sideout battle was nearly the whole story. Passing was not: the better-passing team won only 60% of the time. The teams that won converted the first ball, whatever the pass looked like. Wisconsin’s sweep at Purdue is the clearest example below.

SEC

Kentucky is the only SEC team at 2-0, after a 3-1 win at Vanderbilt and a sweep of Texas. Against Texas it sided out 70.8%, passed 71.4% good and had a 42.6% kill rate to Texas’s 35.4%. Across both matches Kentucky had a 47.2% kill rate, third best of any team in these seven leagues with two charted league matches.

Texas A&M had the most lopsided SEC match of the weekend, sweeping South Carolina with a 79.2% sideout rate and a 51.1% kill rate while holding the Gamecocks to 26.0%. Florida swept at Oklahoma with a 47.5% kill rate.

The upsets: Texas won in five at Tennessee as a 14% underdog, then was swept at Kentucky two days later. Ole Miss swept at Alabama and Missouri beat Vanderbilt, each at 21% pregame.

Big Ten

Five teams are 2-0: Indiana and Nebraska (both 6-0 in sets), Illinois, Wisconsin and Washington (all 6-1). The Big Ten had six upsets, the most of any league, and the sideout rule held in all 18 matches.

Wisconsin swept at Purdue as a 21% underdog. It passed worse than Purdue (50.0% good to 53.5%) and still sided out 66.1% to Purdue’s 47.9%. Washington won at Michigan State, also at 21%, then swept Michigan. Its serve did the work: 55.2% serve KO and a 9.2% ace rate across the two matches. Indiana swept UCLA and Maryland while passing 61.1% good with a 43.0% kill rate.

Penn State and Michigan State split the other big results. Penn State won at Michigan as an underdog, then lost at Michigan State.

Big 12

Kansas (6-0), TCU and Utah (6-1) and Iowa State (6-4) are 2-0.

Kansas was the most complete. Its serve KO rate of 57.9% was 10.6 points above its non-conference number, and it held Houston to 31.0% good passing in a sweep. TCU won with defense and serve, holding opponents to a 30.6% kill rate and acing 9.6% of its serves. Iowa State is 2-0 on two five-setters and lost the sideout battle in both, the only team in these leagues to do that twice.

The upset of the week in the Big 12 was West Virginia over Arizona State in five, at 6% pregame. Houston also won at Kansas State as a 21% underdog.

ACC

Three teams opened 6-0 in sets: Louisville, Pittsburgh and Georgia Tech. SMU (6-1) and North Carolina (6-3) are also 2-0.

Louisville passed 65.5% good, had a 46.0% kill rate and held opponents to a 26.7% kill rate. Georgia Tech sided out 70.1% and held opponents to 25.9%. SMU had the best offense in any of these leagues. Its 53.1% kill rate and 73.0% sideout rate both led every team with two charted league matches.

Virginia Tech’s win at Clemson, at 18% pregame, was the ACC’s biggest surprise.

Big East

It went chalk: the favorite won all 11 matches. Marquette (6-0), Creighton, DePaul, Butler (6-1) and Xavier (6-2) are all 2-0.

Marquette held opponents to a 31.0% kill rate. Creighton’s serve KO rate of 52.1% was 12.5 points above its non-conference number. DePaul passed 56.4% good, 10.2 points better than before league play, and aced 12.3% of its serves.

WCC

Saint Mary’s won in five at San Diego as a 4% underdog, the biggest upset in any of the seven leagues. Saint Mary’s, Loyola Marymount and Pepperdine are all 2-0, and all three won both matches 6-3 in sets. Loyola Marymount did it by siding out 67.5%, eight points above its non-conference rate.

Mountain West

Hawaii swept New Mexico twice with the best passing and defense of any team in these seven leagues: 66.4% good passing (12.4 points above its non-conference number) and a 25.1% opponent kill rate. UTEP won twice at Wyoming, siding out 71.7%. Air Force and UC Davis are also 2-0.

Nevada beat Grand Canyon in five as a 12% underdog, then lost the rematch the next night, also in five.

The fine print
