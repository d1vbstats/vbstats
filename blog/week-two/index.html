Week two: Nebraska pulls away, and the bubble moves

The second weekend of league play in the seven conferences we track, and what it did to our projected NCAA tournament field: three new hosts, a new bubble, and a bigger favorite.

Two weekends of conference play are done. Here’s what happened in the seven leagues we track, and what it did to the Bracketology projection.

The weekend in numbers

From Sept. 30 to Oct. 4 there were 96 conference matches in the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC, Big East, WCC and Mountain West. Fifty-one were sweeps and 12 went five. Home teams won 57 of 96 after going 44-47 in week one, and the pregame favorite won 73 of the 93 matches that had one. That left 20 upsets, at least two in every league.

The order is the same as week one: the attack and the sideout decide matches, and serving and passing come a clear step behind. Eight teams won despite losing the sideout battle, and six of them needed five sets. Marquette’s win at Creighton is the best example. It sided out 54.1% to Creighton’s 58.4% and still won the fifth set 19-17.

Bracketology, week two

The top line held, but the gap grew

The four projected No. 1 seeds are still Nebraska, Pittsburgh, Louisville and Kentucky. What changed is the title race. Nebraska now wins the championship in 48% of our simulations, up from 37% a week ago. Pittsburgh dropped from 37% to 26%, Louisville is at 14% and Kentucky at 8%. No one else is above 1%.

Nebraska swept at Penn State and at Maryland. It sided out 72.9%, had a 45.9% kill rate and aced 11.0% of its serves, and it is 12-0 in sets in Big Ten play. Pittsburgh is also 12-0 in sets, so this isn’t a knock on the Panthers. A title chance depends on everyone else in the bracket, and right now Nebraska is the team the model trusts most in a one-match shootout.

Kentucky held its No. 1 seed despite losing in five at Florida on Sunday.

The top 16 host the first two rounds. This week three teams moved in and three moved out:

Oregon beat Purdue in five and Illinois in four. Vanderbilt swept at LSU and at Ole Miss. Texas A&M swept Alabama and Mississippi State and is the only SEC team that hasn’t dropped a set in league play (9-0). Going the other way, Kansas was swept at home by BYU, Indiana lost in five at Wisconsin, and Purdue lost both matches of its Pacific Northwest trip.

The bottom of the host line is close. Vanderbilt hosts in 48% of our runs and Texas A&M in 45%. Kansas (45%), Michigan State (41%), Baylor (37%), Penn State (33%) and Indiana (31%) are all within reach. Above them, Wisconsin, SMU and Florida host in at least 97% of runs.

Missouri fell the furthest. It was in 66% of our fields a week ago and is at 24% now, after losing at Ole Miss as an 86% favorite and in five at LSU. San Diego went the other way, from the last four in at 56% to 73%. Harvard slipped from the last four in to the first four out.

The last at-large team in now sits around No. 43 in the RPI, and in 80% of runs the cutoff falls between No. 40 and No. 46. Twenty-two teams make the field in at least 99% of runs, up from 18. There are 2,191 D1 matches left to play.

Around the leagues

SEC

Texas A&M, Florida and Georgia are the SEC’s three unbeaten teams at 3-0. Florida beat Kentucky 22-25, 29-27, 25-21, 21-25, 15-8 as a 30% underdog. Florida passed 56.4% good to Kentucky’s 39.2% and held the Wildcats to a .122 hitting percentage. Georgia beat Arkansas and Oklahoma, both in four, and its serve did the work: a 52.4% serve KO rate and a 10.8% ace rate. Texas had the league’s best offense of the week, siding out 67.4% with a 43.5% kill rate.

The upsets were Ole Miss over Missouri (14% pregame), Florida over Kentucky (30%) and LSU over Missouri in five (40%).

Big Ten

Nebraska and Wisconsin are 4-0. Wisconsin beat Indiana in five, then won at Minnesota as a slight underdog. It sided out 66.1% across the two matches. Michigan State swept at UCLA, then won in five at USC, with a 45.6% kill rate and a 55.0% serve KO rate.

Four Big Ten upsets: Illinois swept at Washington (30% pregame), Oregon beat Purdue and Rutgers swept at Ohio State (both 40%), and Wisconsin won at Minnesota (45%). Northwestern (0-4, 1-12 in sets) is the league’s only winless team.

Big 12

TCU (4-0) and BYU (3-0) are the only unbeaten teams. BYU swept at Kansas as a 34% underdog with a 44.1% kill rate across its two road wins. TCU beat Colorado and UCF in four and led the league in sideout rate (67.5%).

The upset of the week was Colorado at Baylor, a 3-1 win at 14% pregame. Houston won at West Virginia and Iowa State won at Arizona, both at 45%.

ACC

Four teams are 4-0: Louisville and Pittsburgh (both 12-0 in sets), SMU (12-1) and North Carolina (12-5). Louisville swept Notre Dame twice, siding out 73.7%, the best of any team in these leagues with two matches this week. Pittsburgh’s serve KO rate was 64.6%. SMU hit .364 with a 45.7% kill rate. North Carolina came back to beat Georgia Tech in five at 40% pregame.

Florida State and Miami each swept NC State at home, both as underdogs.

Big East

Six teams are tied at 3-1: Creighton, Marquette, Butler, Xavier, St. John’s and DePaul. After a week one where every favorite won, there were two upsets: DePaul beat Marquette in five at 45%, and St. John’s beat Xavier 16-14 in the fifth at 35%. Georgetown and Villanova both went 2-0, and Georgetown held opponents to a 28.9% kill rate.

WCC

Loyola Marymount (4-0) is alone at the top after sweeping Saint Mary’s and Pacific with a 71.2% sideout rate and a 10.3% ace rate. Saint Mary’s bounced back from that sweep to win at Pepperdine at 7% pregame, the biggest upset of the week in any of these leagues. It has now produced the WCC’s biggest upset two weeks running. Santa Clara also swept at Portland (40%) and sided out 71.5% for the week.

Mountain West

Hawaii (12-0 in sets) and UC Davis are both 4-0. Hawaii swept Grand Canyon twice on the road, and UC Davis held Air Force to a 28.0% kill rate across two wins. New Mexico had the best serve of any team in these leagues, with a 65.5% serve KO rate. UNLV’s sweep at UTEP, at 21% pregame, was the league’s biggest surprise.
